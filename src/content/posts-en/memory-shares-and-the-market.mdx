SK Hynix closed at 2,186,000 won on 9 July 2026 and at 1,322,000 won on 30 July, a fall of
**39.5 per cent** in fifteen trading days. Samsung Electronics fell 27.4 per cent over the same
stretch. Korean integrated-circuit exports in July 2026 were $32.72bn, the highest month in the
series and 166 per cent above July 2025. The obvious reading is that the market had turned against
memory ahead of the data. The obvious reading is wrong, and one line of arithmetic disposes of it.

## An R-squared of 93.9 per cent and a beta of 1.59

Regressing each share's daily return on the KOSPI daily return over the 33 available observations
gives the following.

<div class="table-scroll">

| 8 Jul to 26 Aug 2026 | Beta to KOSPI | R-squared | Peak-to-trough | Peak to 26 Aug |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Samsung Electronics | 1.35 | **94.3%** | −27.4% | −8.2% |
| SK Hynix | 1.59 | **93.9%** | −39.5% | −22.8% |
| KOSPI | 1.00 | — | −25.2% | −8.9% |

</div>

*Source: Financial Services Commission, Bank of Korea. Own calculation over 33 daily returns.*

An R-squared of 93.9 per cent means that almost all of the daily movement in SK Hynix over this
window is accounted for by the movement of the index it sits in. What is left for every
semiconductor-specific cause combined — pricing, inventory, a competitor's results, an export
figure — is the remaining six per cent.

The beta explains the rest of the difference. At 1.59, SK Hynix moves roughly half again as far as
the index in both directions. An index that fell 25.2 per cent peak to trough would carry a
1.59-beta share down about 40 per cent on that relationship alone, which is what happened.

The six per cent left over does have a direction, and it is worth stating precisely because it is
the part most likely to be over-read. Samsung ran 0.151 percentage points a day ahead of what its
beta implied and SK Hynix 0.180 points behind, which compounds to roughly plus five and minus six
per cent across the window and accounts for the gap between their peak-to-date figures. Neither
estimate survives its own standard error: the t-statistics are 0.48 and −0.47, against 22.6 and 21.8
for the betas. The market relationship is measured firmly here. The company-specific part is not
measured at all.

## The date is the stronger evidence

A regression can be argued with. The calendar is harder to argue with: the KOSPI, Samsung
Electronics and SK Hynix all reached their low on **30 July 2026**, the same trading day. On 28 July
the index fell 10.8 per cent, Samsung 13.4 per cent and SK Hynix 14.6 per cent. On 31 July the index
rose 17.9 per cent, Samsung 26.8 per cent and SK Hynix 30.0 per cent.

A cause specific to memory would have to have arrived and departed on precisely the days the whole
Korean market moved, and would have to have moved the index too. The simpler account is that one
market-wide event moved everything and the higher-beta names moved further.

This is the same conclusion the export data reaches from the other side. Chip exports rose through
the entire drawdown, and the equipment imports that would signal a producer's own change of view
[plateaued rather than fell](/en/posts/chip-equipment-imports/). Neither series recorded the summer.

## What to watch

Whether the relationship holds outside this window. One 34-day sample containing a 25 per cent index
drawdown is a period in which correlations rise mechanically, and betas measured through a crash
tend to overstate what they will be in a calm quarter. The figure to re-run is the same regression
over the next quarter, and a materially lower R-squared would mean the shares had begun trading on
their own news again.

Until then the practical consequence stands: over this summer, the Korean memory tape carried
almost no information about Korean memory. Anyone reading those prices as a forecast of the export
figures was reading the index.
